How I'm building Accord

Accord is a fragrance intelligence app — your scent wardrobe, learned. Weather, mood, and how you actually wear what you own get translated into a daily recommendation. Think a wardrobe app's logging discipline with a sneaker-drop app's visual energy, but the differentiator is the intelligence: it learns preferences, spots gaps in the wardrobe, and understands usage.

ACCORD

The spark

I wanted a tiny ritual that answered, “What should I wear today?” without making me scroll through everything I own. That grew into a full system: a Galaxy (the wardrobe), Vibes (daily reads), Orbits (how often a scent comes around), and a Scent Forecast that reacts to real live weather.

Update — July 3, 2026

A big design-and-data day. The home screen got radically simplified, the wishlist became actionable, and the whole app picked up a real color system and logo.

Before
  • Home was a long scroll — giant two-line headline, text-link actions, pairing cards always visible
  • The “vibe” pill read like a fragrance note; tooltips clipped off-screen
  • Pairing suggestions were abstract text cards
  • Wishlist was view-only; buying a bottle meant re-adding it by hand
  • Wardrobe map was a plain dot-scatter chart
  • One accent color (acid yellow) doing every job
After
  • Home fits one frame: the scent of the day dominates, you choose it (“Make it today's scent”), and pairing recs appear only after you commit
  • Two-tone label:value vibe pill; tooltips render through a portal and can never be clipped
  • Pairings are real bottles from your own wardrobe, picked by a complementary-family map
  • “SNAGGED IT” moves a wish straight into the wardrobe; a new A–Z Houses index spans everything you own and want
  • The map is now a floating collage of actual bottle cutouts — sized by wear count, glowing in their scent-family hue, tappable
  • A color-role system: yellow = action & identity, blue = intelligence, magenta = desire/wishlist — plus six scent-family hues as the data layer

Under the hood, the fragrance atlas got richer too: note pyramids, seasons, and sillage were backfilled for the whole wardrobe, and 30 wishlist bottles auto-linked to verified retail pages. Screenshots of the new screens are coming — the floating bottle map is my favorite thing in the app right now.

Design influences

Accord's look — internally we call it the acid wardrobe — is deliberately borrowed from places fragrance apps never look:

  • GOAT (the sneaker marketplace): the floating background-removed product hero over expressive graphics, the stat bubble under the object, the dense zoomable collection grid, and browse-by-collection tiles — reinterpreted as the bottle hero, vibe pill, Galaxy grid, and Houses index.
  • Whering (digital wardrobe): the logging discipline — wear counts, a calendar ritual, and cost-per-wear as a sustainability signal.
  • Acid-grunge editorial graphics: neon acids on near-black, grain, poster typography — the reason to open the app should feel like a fashion drop, not a database.
  • Type: Anton for the poster voice, Space Grotesk for display, JetBrains Mono for labels — all open fonts, nothing licensed. Every aura graphic and backdrop is CSS/SVG, generated in-house.

Built with Claude Fable 5

This build cycle ran on Claude Code with Claude Fable 5, Anthropic's new Mythos-class model, and the difference showed up in the work itself. The tooltip bug is a good example: instead of nudging pixels, it traced the clipping to the swipeable rail's scroll container and rewrote the component to render through a portal — a root-cause fix, first try. Design feedback like “I keep thinking Vibe is a Note” came back as a systemic answer (a two-tone label:value pill plus a color-role system for the whole app), and eight separate pieces of screen feedback landed as one coherent single-frame redesign across five files, verified live in the browser before I ever saw it. Faster, but more importantly: fewer rounds of “close, but not quite.”

What is next

Swipe actions (swipe a wish to snag it, swipe a bottle to log a wear), then the comeback layer: streaks, a weekly recap, and calendar energy feeding the daily pull. After that — community, native barcode scanning, and the app stores.
